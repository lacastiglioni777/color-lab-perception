A CLASSROOM-FRIENDLY MINI-LAB

Color Perception Lab

Can you trust your eyes? Eight challenges inspired by Josef Albers, plus a build-your-own color comparison studio.

Grades 6–12+ Art · STEAM · perception 10–15 minutes

Learning goals

  • Investigate how color context affects perception.
  • Distinguish between colors that appear different and colors with different RGB values.
  • Justify a prediction by making a direct side-by-side comparison.
  • Design an original visual perception experiment.

Suggested 12-minute sequence

  • 2 min: Introduce Josef Albers and ask: “Can we always trust what we see?”
  • 7 min: Play the eight rounds. Students predict SAME or DIFFERENT, then discuss the reveal.
  • 3 min: Open the Studio. Have students make identical gray centers appear different against contrasting backgrounds.

Projector mode and classroom controls

Open the website, scroll to The Challenge, and turn on Projector mode. Disable point scoring if students are investigating for curiosity rather than competing. At the end of the game, select Make your own illusion to exit Projector Mode and open the Studio. Students can also use keyboard shortcuts: S for same, D for different, and Enter to reveal/continue.

Discussion prompts

What changed when the backgrounds faded away? Did the actual colors change, or only your perception?

Why would this effect be useful to painters, designers, photographers, or filmmakers?

Extension: invent an illusion

In The Studio, keep the center swatches identical and change both backgrounds. Students should create one combination that makes the center colors look strongly different. Ask them to sketch, describe, or demonstrate their result.

Accuracy and accessibility notes

The challenge mixes genuinely identical centers with moderately different centers; the reveals are based on the actual RGB values used. Screens, color settings, vision differences, and room lighting can affect perception. Avoid requiring every student to see the same effect. The site uses no flashing lights, collects no student data, and honors reduced-motion browser settings.

How to run or publish

Open index.html on a computer to use the site offline. To publish, upload the complete website folder or ZIP to a static website host such as Netlify Drop. A custom domain is not required. Keep your downloaded copy as a backup.
